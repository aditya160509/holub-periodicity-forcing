# Holub periodicity-forcing problem: new phase-comparison theorems

## Result and scope

This is a continuation of the supplied `holub-dbound-pack.zip`, specifically its request for a length-difference reduction or direct proofs for whole slices. All new proof claims below are candidate analytical theorems pending independent proof review. The original manuscript's periodic-morphism reduction, balanced-prefix theorem and seed (1,0,1,0) are used as dependencies, not claimed as new.

For words over an arbitrary alphabet consider

$$X^iY^2X^kYX^\ell=U^iV^2U^kVU^\ell,$$

where $k\ge1$ and $i+\ell\ge1$. A tuple forces periodicity if every solution has identical substitutions ($X=U$, $Y=V$) or all four words are powers of one word. Holub's admissibility condition is $|V|\ge\max(|X|,|Y|,|U|)$.

**New main theorem (unrestricted).** The following entire regions force periodicity, without imposing the admissibility condition:

1. $\ell=0$, $i,k\ge1$, $k\ne 2i$.
2. $i=0$, $\ell\ge1$, $k>2\ell$.
3. $i,k,\ell\ge1$, $k\ge2$, $i+k>2\ell$, $i<2(k+\ell)$.

The zero-tail exception $k=2i$ is genuinely nonforcing, including under Holub's length condition, by the supplied family's D explicit witnesses. In particular the large-middle-block slice $k>2(i+\ell)$ is entirely settled. The third region encompasses every strictly nested middle block with positive outer exponents and $k\ge2$. The proof section subdivides this region into overlapping subtheorems; the simplified statement above has the same scope.

The proof is independent of the magnitudes of the word lengths. There is no cutoff on $d=|X|-|U|$. This replaces a difficult finite-bound approach for these families with a direct argument.

## Main mathematical mechanism

Orient the two substitutions so $d=x-u>0$ and let $D=v-y=(i+k+\ell)d/3$. Let $X=R^t$ with primitive $R$ of length $r$. Put $L=2D-id$ and $Q=D-\ell d$. When both are nonnegative, the middle $U^k$ lies inside $X^k$. Factor

$$X^i=U^iC,\qquad X^\ell=BU^\ell,\qquad X^k=PU^kA.$$

The key exact identity is

$$ V=AYB,\qquad CY^2P=(AYB)^2. $$

Thus $C,A$ are prefix-comparable and $P,B$ suffix-comparable. Extending the comparisons by the common $U$ runs and applying uniqueness of primitive rotations gives congruences for $H=Q-id$ and $F=L-\ell d$. Since $F-H=D$, both congruences imply $r\mid D$. This aligns the separate $U$ runs in the same periodic word, forces $U$ to be an $R$-power, and then forces $Y,V$ to be $R$-powers by the two-word code property.

In unbalanced regions only one phase congruence is available. Fine–Wilf and divisor bounds often suffice; the harder regimes use literal cancellation, transport through powers of $R$, and an exponent-count homomorphism on the free subgroup generated by $R,U$. If those two words fail to commute, their independent exponent counts contradict the equation. The short exceptional regimes are excluded using cyclic Hamming-distance invariance, including the zero-head case $(i,k,\ell)=(0,4,1)$.

The complete proofs are in `proof.tex`, written as a Section 10 insertion for the original manuscript. The Markdown report includes a text conversion of that section. The supplied original Sections 1–9 are not re-proved in this continuation.

## What is still open here

The complete Holub classification is **not** solved. No linear or polynomial global bound on d was found. The original fixed-d shortening argument and gcd reduction do not handle arbitrary primitive length vectors, and the general word-equation bounds discussed in the supplied pack remain impractically large. The 120 remaining tuples through cap six include:

- The zero-head cases $i=0$, $k\le2\ell$ not already classified.
- Non-nested middle regions ($L<0$ or $Q<0$) with both outer exponents positive.
- The two strictly nested positive-outer rays with $k=1$, namely $i=2\ell$ and $i=2\ell+1$, after removing any already-known cases.

Boundary cases $L=0$ or $Q=0$ in the positive-outer region fall under the supplied balanced-prefix theorem, except its known nonforcing families. The explicit lists are authoritative for finite exponent caps.

The two k=1 rays are a particularly targeted next proof problem: here $L+Q=d$, so neither overlap is long enough to guarantee the primitive phase congruences by the present length argument. The zero-head non-nested region is another natural target. The phase/cancellation technique was chosen over an attempted global Euclidean descent because no valid descent for gcd-one length vectors was established. No blanket claim is made that the remaining cases have been ruled out.

# Full mathematical proof section

We work with the equation $$\tag{1}
 X^iY^2X^kYX^\ell=U^iV^2U^kVU^\ell,
 \qquad k\ge1,\quad i+\ell\ge1.$$ No maximum-length condition is imposed. The result below gives direct proofs in infinite exponent regions, rather than a bound on the length of a counterexample. The remaining exponent triples are not settled here.

**Main theorem**.  Equation (1) forces periodicity in each of the following cases:

1.  $\ell=0$, $i,k\ge1$, and $k\ne2i$;

2.  $i=0$, $\ell\ge1$, and $k>2\ell$;

3.  $i,k,\ell\ge1$, $2k>i+\ell$, and $i+k>2\ell$;

4.  $i,k,\ell\ge1$, $k\ge2$, $i+k>2\ell$, and $2k+2\ell>i$.

The only exception on the first slice is $k=2i$, for which family D in Theorem original Theorem 7 supplies a counterexample satisfying Holub's length condition. In particular the whole slice $k>2(i+\ell)$ with $i+\ell>0$ is forcing, even without that condition.

## Preliminaries and notation

We may exchange the two substitutions and assume $d=x-u>0$; otherwise equal lengths give identical substitutions. Put $S=i+k+\ell$, $D=v-y=Sd/3>0$. By Lemma original periodic-morphism lemma, none of these tuples can have a counterexample with a periodic morphism. In particular all four images are nonempty in a counterexample. (Every empty image makes its morphism periodic.) Equivalently, the same reduction follows from the Spehner binary-code power theorem: the pattern is primitive, has three $b$'s and at least two $a$'s, and the image under a nonperiodic morphism cannot be a proper power. The exceptional seed $i=k=1,\ell=0$ is already covered by Theorem original three-rays theorem.

Let $X=R^t$ with $R$ primitive, $r=|R|>0$, $t\ge1$. Index the bi-infinite periodic word $R^{\mathbb Z}$ so that position zero begins a copy of $R$. By the *phase* of a factor we mean its starting or ending position modulo $r$, as specified. Two equal factors of length at least $r$ have the same starting phase (and the same ending phase), since the $r$ rotations of a primitive word are distinct. We will compare factors of separate powers of $X$ in this coordinate system.

We use two classical facts. First, if a factor of $R^{\mathbb Z}$ equals $U^m$ and has length at least $r+u-\gcd(r,u)$, then Fine--Wilf gives period $\gcd(r,u)$. Its length is at least $r$ and $u$, so the primitive roots of $U$ and $R$ are conjugate and $r\mid u$. If $U$ is also a prefix or suffix of $X$, those roots are equal, hence $U\in R^*$. This prefix or suffix property holds in (1) as $i+\ell>0$ and $x>u$. Second, two noncommuting words freely generate a rank-two subgroup of the ambient free group (see Hadravová–Holub (2015), Lemma 1). Thus, if $R,U$ do not commute, on $\langle R,U\rangle$ there is a homomorphism $\nu$ to $\mathbb Z$ with $\nu(R)=0$, $\nu(U)=1$. We use this exponent-count homomorphism only after expressing all relevant words as group products of $R$ and $U$. All original word equalities remain literal monoid equalities; inverses are used only in these auxiliary group computations.

#### Transport of a prefix or suffix.

If $B$ is a positive power of $R$ and $Y$ is a prefix of $BY$, then $Y$ is a prefix of $R^\omega$, and $BY=YZ$ where $Z$ is a power of the rotation of $R$ at phase $y$. The prefix assertion follows by repeatedly using $Y[j]=Y[j-|B|]$ for $j\ge |B|$; the equation and rotation follow by reading $BY$ on $R^\omega$. Dually, if $Y$ is a suffix of $YB$, then $Y$ is a suffix of a sufficiently long power of $R$, and $YB=ZY$ where $Z$ is a power of the rotation of $R$ at phase $-y$.

## An exact cancellation identity in the nested region

Put $$L=2D-id,\quad Q=D-\ell d,\quad
 H=Q-id=\frac{k-2(i+\ell)}3d,\quad
 F=L-\ell d=\frac{2k-i-\ell}3d.$$ Here $F-H=D$. If $L,Q\ge0$, the middle $U^k$ lies wholly inside the middle $X^k$ (the left minus right boundary displacements are $id,id-D,id-2D,(i+k)d-2D,-\ell d,0$). Define words $C,B,P,A$ by $$\tag{2}
 X^i=U^i C,\qquad X^\ell=B U^\ell,\qquad
 X^k=P U^k A.$$ Their lengths are $id,\ell d,L,Q$, respectively. For zero outer exponents the corresponding word is empty. Comparing suffixes of the common expansion and cancelling $U^\ell$ gives $V=AYB$. Cancelling the prefix $U^i$ and the common suffix $U^k AYB U^\ell$ gives $$\tag{3}
 C Y^2 P=(A Y B)^2.$$ In particular $C,A$ are prefix-comparable and $P,B$ are suffix-comparable. These comparisons are of literal words, independent of the size of $Y$.

**Phase lemma**.  In the nested region, if $i>0$ and $$\min(iu,ku)+\min(id,Q)\ge r,$$ then $r\mid H$. If $\ell>0$ and $$\min(\ell u,ku)+\min(\ell d,L)\ge r,$$ then $r\mid F$.

*Proof.* For the first comparison extend the matching prefixes of $C,A$ on the left by the common suffix of $U^i,U^k$, of length $\min(iu,ku)$. The resulting two equal factors of powers of $X$ have phases that differ by $(-Q)-(-id)=-H$. Their length is at least $r$. For the second, extend the matching suffixes of $P,B$ on the right by the common prefix of $U^k,U^\ell$. Their ending phases differ by $L-\ell
d=F$. Apply primitive rotation uniqueness. ◻

**Propagation lemma**.  In the nested region, either $r\mid D$ or $XU=UX$ suffices to force periodicity.

*Proof.* If $r\mid D$, then $r\mid Sd$ and hence $r\mid Su$ since $r\mid x$. The factor $U^i$ starts at phase zero, $U^k$ at phase $L\equiv iu$ (modulo $r$), and $U^\ell$ starts at phase $-\ell u\equiv(i+k)u$. For zero exponents the corresponding condition is vacuous. Therefore $U^S$ equals the prefix of $R^\omega$ of length $Su$, which is a positive power of $R$. Uniqueness of primitive roots gives $U\in R^*$. Conversely, if $XU=UX$, then $r\mid u,d$ and the occurrence of the full word $U^k$ at phase $L$ yields $r\mid L$. So $r\mid2D$ and $r\mid3D=Sd$, whence $r\mid D$.

In either case $r$ divides $Q$ and $\ell d$. Thus $V=AYB=R^{Q/r}Y R^{\ell d/r}$. If $R,Y$ do not commute they form a two-word code, and substituting this expression into (1) gives two unequal sequences of codewords: the left has adjacent $Y$ tokens while the first two $Y$ tokens on the right are separated by $(Q+\ell d)/r=D/r>0$ copies of $R$. Injectivity is contradicted. Consequently $Y,V\in R^*$ too. ◻

## Positive outer exponents

Suppose $i,k,\ell\ge1$. First assume $Q,L\ge d$. Both lengths in Lemma Phase lemma are at least $u+d=x\ge r$. Hence $r\mid H,F$, so $r\mid F-H=D$, and Lemma Propagation lemma applies. This proves a useful subregion of part 4 of Theorem Main theorem.

For part 3, put $c=i+\ell$, $M=2k-c>0$. We have $Q>0$ and $L=F+\ell d>\ell d$, so the nested identity applies. The second comparison in Lemma Phase lemma has length at least $u+d=x$, whence $r\mid F=Md/3$. In particular $r\mid Md$, and $r\mid Mu$. If $M\le k-1$, Fine--Wilf on $U^k$ forces $U\in R^*$, since $ku\ge r+u$. If $M=k$ and $r<ku$, then $r$ is a proper divisor of $ku$, so $r\le ku/2\le(k-1)u$ ($k\ge2$). If $M=k$ and $r=ku$, the length-$r$ factor $U^k$ would be an imprimitive rotation of $R$, a contradiction. Thus all cases $M\le k$ are settled.

It remains to consider $M>k$ (equivalently $k>c$) and assume $U\notin R^*$. The same Fine--Wilf argument gives $r>(k-1)u$. Since $c\ge2$, $M\le2(k-1)$; a proper divisor of $Mu$ is at most $(k-1)u$. Consequently $r=Mu$. Since $r\mid F$, the start of $U^k$ has phase $L\equiv\ell d\equiv-\ell u$. As $k>\ell$ and $ku<r$, the portion crossing phase zero consists of $U^\ell$ at the end and $U^{k-\ell}$ at the beginning of $R$. In particular $$R=U^{k-\ell}G U^\ell,
 \qquad |G|=(M-k)u=(k-c)u>0.$$ Here $A$ begins at phase $(k-\ell)u$, so begins with $G$ if its length permits; $C$ begins at phase $iu<(k-\ell)u$, so begins with $U^{k-c}$ if its length permits. We check both lengths explicitly. Since $x\ge r$, $d\ge(M-1)u$, and $id\ge(k-c)u$. If $k\ge2c$, then $Q\ge id$ and hence $Q\ge(k-c)u$. If $k<2c$, then $Q\ge d/3\ge(M-1)u/3\ge(k-c)u$, the last inequality being $k\le2c-1$. Prefix comparability in (3) now gives $G=U^{k-c}$, contradicting the primitivity of $R$ ($M>1$). This proves part 3.

## The rest of the strictly nested region with $k\ge2$

We prove part 4. Thus $Q,L>0$ and $L+Q=kd\ge2d$; at least one of $Q,L$ is at least $d$. The case $F>0$ has just been proved in part 3. If $F=0$, then $i+\ell=2k$ and $S=3k$. The inequality $Q>0$ implies $i>\ell$, so $k<i<2k$ and $i+k>2k$. The balanced-prefix theorem (Theorem original balanced-prefix theorem) applies, and none of its nonforcing exceptions is in this region. Hence we assume $F<0$.

If $L\ge d$, the second comparison in Lemma Phase lemma gives $r\mid F$. Put $M=i+\ell-2k>0$, $m=\max(i,k,\ell)\ge2$. Then $r\mid Mu$. A run of $U^m$ is a factor of a power of $X$ (the outer runs are common prefixes or suffixes; the middle is nested). If $r\le(m-1)u$, Fine--Wilf settles the case. If $M<m$, this is immediate; if $M=m$ and $r=mu$, $U^m$ is a length-$r$ factor of $R^{\mathbb Z}$, impossible by primitivity. Otherwise $M>m$ and $M\le2m-2$ because $k\ge1$. The only divisor of $Mu$ greater than $(m-1)u$ is $Mu$ itself. Thus $r=Mu$. Both $i,\ell<M$, and $R$ has prefix $U^i$ and suffix $U^\ell$. These cover $R$, since $i+\ell=M+2k>M$, and their $u$-letter blocks are aligned because $r=Mu$. This makes $R=U^M$, impossible. We have shown that $r\mid F$ with $F<0$ always suffices in the nested region.

It remains that $Q\ge d$ (in particular if $L<d$). The first comparison gives $r\mid H$. Write $C=AE$, $B=GP$ using comparability. Here $|E|=-H=D-F>D$, $|G|=-F>0$, and $E=R^e$ with $e\ge1$. Cancel $A$ and the terminal $P$ in (3) to obtain $$\tag{4}
 E Y^2=Y B A Y G.$$ Transport $EY=YZ$ with $Y$ a prefix of $R^\omega$, $Z$ a power of the rotation at phase $y$. Then $ZY=B A Y G$. In particular $T=BA$, of length $D<|Z|$, is a prefix of $Z$. If $\ell d\ge r$, the occurrence of $B$ at the start of $T$ and at phase zero in $X^\ell$ gives $r\mid y$. Hence $Y\in R^*$. As $r\mid H$, the phase of $A$ is $-Q\equiv iu$, so $A=U^{-i}R^a$ in the free group. From (2) we have $B=R^{t\ell}U^{-\ell}$ and $P=R^{tk-a}U^{i-k}$. Hence $G=B P^{-1}=R^{t\ell}U^{k-i-\ell}R^{a-tk}$. If $R,U$ do not commute, applying $\nu$ to (4) yields $0=k-2(i+\ell)$, impossible for $F<0$. Otherwise apply Lemma Propagation lemma.

Suppose finally that $\ell d<r$ and $R,U$ do not commute. Since $Q\ge d$, we have $i+k\ge2\ell+3$. If $\ell\ge2$, the largest of $i,k$ is at least four. Fine--Wilf on that run then forces $u<d/2$, so $r\le x<3d/2<\ell d$, a contradiction. Thus $\ell=1$. The conditions $F<0$ and $Q\ge d$ give $i\ge2k$ and $i+k\ge5$, so $i\ge4$. Fine--Wilf on $U^i$ gives both $r>(i-1)u$ and $(i-2)u<d$, so $r\le x<2d$; as $r>d$ and $r\mid x$, $X=R$. The divisor condition $r\mid H$ implies $r\mid M'u$ where $M'=2i+2-k$. Since $M'\le2i+1\le3(i-1)$ for $i\ge4$, the quotient $M'u/r$ is one or two. If two, then $r=(i+1-k/2)u$. For $k\ge4$, this is at most $(i-1)u$, impossible. For $k=3$ the Fine--Wilf threshold is $iu$ exactly ($\gcd(r,u)=u/2$), and for $k=2$ the prefix $U^i$ equals $R$, impossible. For $k=1$ (not needed for part 4, but included in this argument), $r=(i+1/2)u$ and the prefix $U^i$ overlaps the suffix $U$ by $u/2$. The two halves of $U$ are equal, so the prefix and suffix make $R$ a power of this half, again impossible.

If the quotient is one, $r=M'u$, and $R$ has prefix $U^i$ and suffix $U$, so $R=U^i K U$ with $|K|=(i+1-k)u=gu$ say. Here $1\le g\le i$. The phase of $A$ is $iu$, hence $A$ begins with $KU$ (it is a suffix of powers of $R$ with that phase). Also $B$ is $R$ with its final $U$ removed. The factor $T=BA$ of $R^{\mathbb Z}$ therefore begins with $U^i K K U$, of length $r+gu$. Its period $r$ makes its first $gu$ letters, namely $U^g$, equal its letters at positions $r$ through $r+gu-1$, namely the suffix of $K$ after its first $u$ letters followed by $U$. Consequently $K=J U^{g-1}$ with $|J|=u$. The first $r$ letters of $T$ now have the form $U^i J U^{g-1}J$, a cyclic rotation of $R=U^i J U^g$. There are $M'=i+g+1>4$ blocks of length $u$. If $J\ne U$, a rotation of $R$ has at least $M'-2$ blocks equal to the same rotation of $U$, since at most two blocks touch $J$. The proposed rotation has exactly two $J$ blocks and $M'-2>2$ blocks equal to $U$. The repeated rotated block must be $U$, so the rotation fixes $U^{M'}$. Its Hamming distance from $U^{M'}$ must then be invariant, but it changes from $h$ to $2h$, where $h>0$ is the distance between $J,U$. Thus $J=U$ and $R$ is imprimitive, a contradiction. This completes part 4.

## The zero-tail slice $\ell=0$

Assume $i,k\ge1$, $k\ne2i$, and $i+k\ge3$; the excluded small seed was already proved. Then $D\ge d$. First suppose $i\le2k$, so $L\ge0$, $Q=D>0$ and the nested factorization applies. Here $B$ is empty. Lemma Phase lemma gives $r\mid H=D-id$, since its first comparison has length at least $u+d$. Consequently $$\tag{5}
 r\mid (k-2i)d,\qquad r\mid (k-2i)u.$$ If $k>i$, then $0<|k-2i|\le k-1$, giving $r\le(k-1)u$ and Fine--Wilf on $U^k$ followed by Lemma Propagation lemma settles the case.

Now $k\le i\le2k$. Set $C=A E$ (prefix comparison), so $|E|=id-D>0$ is a multiple of $r$. Since $C$ ends at phase zero, $E=R^e$, $e\ge1$. Cancelling $A$ in (3) gives $$\tag{6}
 E Y^2 P=Y A Y.$$ Then $Y$ is a prefix of $EY$; transport gives $EY=YZ$ with $Z$ of phase $y$ and length $|E|$. Cancelling $Y$ shows $ZYP=AY$. As $|E|=id-D\le D=|A|$, $Z$ is a prefix of $A$. Comparing at least $r$ letters gives $y\equiv-D\equiv iu\pmod r$.

Both $Y$ and $U^i$ are prefixes of $R^\omega$ with lengths congruent modulo $r$, and $A$ is a suffix of a power of $R$ of length $D$. In the free group there exist integers $b,a$ such that $$Y=R^b U^i,\qquad A=U^{-i}R^a.$$ Indeed $U^i A$ reads continuously on $R^\omega$ and has length a multiple of $r$. Moreover $X^k=P U^k A$ implies $P=R^{tk-a}U^{i-k}$. If $R,U$ do not commute, apply $\nu$ to (6): $2i+(i-k)=i-i+i$, so $k=2i$, excluded. Thus they commute. Lemma Propagation lemma concludes this nested case.

For $i>2k$, the middle run is not nested. Define $C$ by $X^i=U^i C$ and write $V=AY$ from the final suffix comparison ($Y$ is a suffix of $V$), $|A|=D$. Then $C=A E$ with $|E|=id-D>D>0$. Let $J$ be the left overflow just after the second $Y$ (length $id-2D>0$). Corresponding prefix cancellations give $$\tag{7}
 E Y^2=Y A Y J,\qquad J X^k=U^k A.$$ These also follow by cancelling the common $U^i$ and final $Y$ and comparing the intermediate prefix lengths.

We claim $r\mid |E|$. If $D\ge kx$, then $X^k$ is a suffix of $A$ by (7); this full power occurs in $X^i$ at phase $iu+D-kx$, yielding $r\mid iu+D$, hence $r\mid id-D$. If $D<kx$, then $A$ is a suffix of $X^k$. The word before $A$ in $X^k$ is a suffix of $U^k$, of length $kx-D$, since $J X^k=U^k A$ and $J$ is longer than zero. Compare the two occurrences of $A$ in $X^i$ and $X^k$, extending to the left by $\min(iu,kx-D)$ common letters from $U^i,U^k$; the total length is $\min(D+iu,kx)\ge x$ because $D\ge d$. The phases yield the same conclusion. In either case $E=R^e$ with $e\ge1$, and $A$ is a suffix of a power of $R$ (from $X^i=U^i A E$).

We next show that $D\ge r$ unless $R,U$ already commute. If they do not commute, the common prefix $U^i$ cannot satisfy the Fine--Wilf threshold with periods $r,u$, so $(i-2)u<d$ because $r\le x=u+d$. If $i\ge4$, $x<3d/2$ while $D\ge5d/3$, hence $D>r$. Otherwise $i>2k$ implies $(i,k)=(3,1)$, so $D=4d/3$, $|E|=5d/3$. If $D<r$, divisibility gives $r=5d/3$. Since $x<2d$ and $r\mid x$, $x=r$, $u=2d/3$. Then $3u=r+u-\gcd(r,u)$, the Fine--Wilf threshold, contradicting noncommutation. If $R,U$ already commute, $r\mid d$ and $D\ge d\ge r$. Thus in every case $D\ge r$.

From (7), $Y$ is a prefix of $EY$; again transport $EY=YZ$ with phase $y$. The equality $ZY=AYJ$ and $|Z|>|A|\ge r$ give $y\equiv-D\equiv iu\pmod r$. As before in the free group $Y=R^b U^i$, $A=U^{-i}R^a$, and now $J=U^{k-i}R^{a-tk}$. If $R,U$ do not commute, applying $\nu$ to $E Y^2=Y A Y J$ yields $2i=i-i+i+k-i=k$, impossible. Thus $R,U$ commute. Now $r\mid d$ and $r\mid id-D$ give $r\mid D$; $y\equiv-D$ yields $r\mid y$ and $Y\in R^*$, and $V=AY\in R^*$. This finishes the whole zero-tail slice.

## The zero-head slice $i=0$, $k>2\ell>0$

Now $L=2D$, $Q=(k-2\ell)d/3>0$, $F=(2k-\ell)d/3>0$. The second comparison in Lemma Phase lemma yields $r\mid F$, as $L>\ell d$. By suffix comparability write $P=E B$, $E=R^e$, $e\ge1$. Note $|E|=F=D+Q$. Equation (3) reduces after cancelling $B$ to $$\tag{8}
 Y^2 E=A Y B A Y.$$ Thus $Y$ is a suffix of $YE$. The suffix transport gives $YE=ZY$, where $Z$ is a rotation of $R^e$ of phase $-y$. Cancelling the final $Y$ in (8), we obtain $YZ=A Y T$, $T=BA$. Since $|T|=D<|Z|$, $T$ is a suffix of $Z$ and is a factor of $R^{\mathbb Z}$ ending at phase $-y$.

If $Q=|A|\ge r$, compare $A$ as the suffix of $Z$ and the suffix of $X^k$. This gives $r\mid y$, so $Y,Z$ are powers of $R$. Now $A$ is a prefix of $YZ$ and has length at least $r$, giving $r\mid Q$. Since $r\mid F=D+Q$, we have $r\mid D$, and apply Lemma Propagation lemma.

If $|B|=\ell d\ge r$, compare $B$ as a prefix of $X^\ell$ and as the prefix of $T$ in $Z$ at phase $-y-D$. It gives $y\equiv-D$. Since $r\mid D+Q$, the suffixes $A$ and $Y$ of powers of $R$ have congruent lengths. Therefore $A=Y R^a$ for some integer $a$ in the free group. From $B U^\ell=X^\ell$ and $X^k=E B U^k A$, we also obtain $B=R^{t\ell}U^{-\ell}$ and $A=U^{\ell-k}R^{tk-e-t\ell}$. In particular $Y$ belongs to $\langle R,U\rangle$. If $R,U$ do not commute, apply $\nu$ to (8) to get $2(\ell-k)=4(\ell-k)-\ell$, hence $\ell=2k$, impossible. If they commute, apply Lemma Propagation lemma directly.

It remains to consider $\ell d<r$ and $Q<r$ with $R,U$ not commuting. Fine--Wilf in the nested middle run gives $ku<r+u-\gcd(r,u)$; hence $(k-2)u<d$. If $\ell\ge2$ then $k\ge5$ and $x<3d/2<\ell d$, contradiction. Thus $\ell=1$, $k\ge3$. Now $d<r\le x<2d$, so $X=R$ (a proper divisor of $x$ is at most $x/2<d$). Because $r\mid F=(2k-1)d/3$ and $r\mid x$, we have $r\mid(2k-1)u$. Since $r>(k-1)u$, the quotient $(2k-1)u/r$ is one or two. If two, $r=(k-1/2)u$ with $u$ even and $\gcd(r,u)=u/2$, giving $ku=r+u-\gcd(r,u)$, a contradiction. If one, $r=(2k-1)u$, $d=(2k-2)u$ and $F/r=(2k-2)/3$ is integral. So $k\equiv1$ modulo three. For $k\ge7$, $Q=2(k-1)(k-2)u/3\ge(2k-1)u=r$, contradiction. Only $k=4$ remains.

Here $r=x=7u$, $d=6u$, $D=10u$, $Q=4u$, $F=14u$. The middle $U^4$ starts at phase $-u$, so $R$ begins with $U^3$ and ends with $U$. Since $T=BA$ has period $7u$ and length $10u$, its first and last $3u$ letters agree. Its first $3u$ letters are $U^3$ (a prefix of $B$), its last $3u$ letters are a suffix of $R$. Thus $R=U^3 K U^3$ with $|K|=u$. The length-$7u$ prefix of $T=U^3 K U^2 K U^3$ must be a cyclic rotation of $R$.

For completeness, if $U\ne K$ this is impossible: a rotation of $U^3 K U^3$ read in seven blocks of size $u$ has at least five blocks that are the same rotation of $U$, since at most two blocks touch the unique $K$ block (including cyclic wraparound). But the proposed rotation $U^3 K U^2 K$ has exactly five $U$ blocks and two $K$ blocks. The repeated rotated block must equal $U$, so this rotation fixes $U^7$. Cyclic Hamming distance to $U^7$ is invariant under it. The two words have distances $h$ and $2h$, respectively, where $h$ is the positive Hamming distance between $K$ and $U$. This is a contradiction. Consequently $K=U$, and $R=U^7$ is imprimitive. The final exceptional length regime is thereby eliminated. This completes the proof of Theorem Main theorem.

#### Status.

Intersecting Theorem Main theorem with the previously unresolved lists removes $27$ of $63$ tuples through exponent four, and $90$ of $210$ through exponent six. The remaining counts are $36$ and $120$, respectively. These are unconditional proofs for those removed tuples, subject to ordinary independent mathematical review; no bound on $d$ was needed. The other residual tuples, and a polynomial bound on $d$ for them, remain open in this work.
