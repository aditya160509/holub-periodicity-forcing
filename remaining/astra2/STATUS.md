# Bounds on the length difference

Let S = i+k+ℓ. For every residual tuple an unrestricted counterexample, if one exists, has an unrestricted witness of difference at most

    n = 1000(S+10), q = 1000n, N = (100q)^(4q), d₀ = (204n)^N.

On the entire residual slice k > 2(i+ℓ), an admissible counterexample has an **admissible** witness within this same bound. These results and the fixed-d shortening lemma give finite tests for the stated witness problems at each input tuple. The bound is doubly exponential and the computation only goes to 12. No tuple is discharged at all differences. The remaining open counts are **63 through exponent four and 210 through exponent six**. Four and twelve of these tuples, respectively, belong to the slice with the admissible cutoff.

## Proof and limitations

* The unrestricted cutoff specializes the monoid case of Ciobanu, Diekert and Elder, Theorem 4. The input fixes a proper prefix or suffix and marks an actual mismatch in a commutator. The proof counts states using the authors' explicit alphabet and equation-length bounds. Cycle deletion on the resulting finite graph preserves both marked properties. The resulting numerical cutoff is derived here from their construction. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.02149)
* For k > 2(i+ℓ), the middle right run lies entirely in the middle left run. A long middle overlap synchronizes their primitive roots. Reading the suffix then expresses V in the two-word code generated by that root and Y, which forces commutation. For k ≥ 4 this covers all oriented solutions failing v ≥ x. For k = 3 every remaining admissible counterexample has X as a prefix of a rotation of V. Two extra word equations express precisely the rotation certificate, preserving the length condition along the automaton path.
* Elsewhere, a positive unrestricted test could produce a witness with v < x. The proof does not give a bound on the smallest admissible witness there. An empty unrestricted test through d₀ would still prove Holub forcing. No such exhaustive test through d₀ has been run.
* Common-divisor compression is a genuine reduction in d and preserves admissibility. It leaves gcd-one vectors. The endpoint-order cell heuristic already fails on a nonforcing control with two gcd-one admissible vectors in the same cell. Proof.tex gives both vectors and an explicit binary witness. A refinement by other hyperplanes could still work.
* The finer fixed-d cut and the middle-block criterion strengthen the earlier overlap bounds. The full-box computation also runs without either pruning rule.

## Verification

The C++ solver normalizes by the gcd, streams the equation's occurrence intervals, and joins equal letter positions. The normalized component criterion is proved in proof.tex. For caps 4 and 6 the scanner runs both the original unrestricted box and the extra part of the original Holub box. The result tables record both modes per tuple per d, with zero visits when 3 does not divide Sd. The Python summarizer independently derives the expected rectangles and counts their admissible lattice points. The cap-4 table overlaps the cap-6 table.

The independent Python checker expands the words literally and tests all pairwise commutators on the most general solution for nonforcing controls. Its concrete local checks include nonunary periodic cores, a nonunary middle-block primitive root, and literal tests of the two edge-case and branch encodings. See checks/audit-final.log. General finite-automaton completeness comes from the cited theorem.

Reproduce with a C++17 compiler. Compile checks/scan.cpp with -O3. For each cap run the binary with arguments CAP, CAP full, and CAP extra, writing throughCAP.csv, fullCAP.csv and extraCAP.csv in checks/. Run 2 controls to generate controls.csv. Then run checks/independent.py and checks/summarize.py. The scanner retains one union-find array, independent of the number of length vectors visited.

The completed scan found zero counterexamples in both modes. Through exponent four it checked 17,343,350 unrestricted vectors and 18,761,096 admissible vectors. Through exponent six it checked 132,249,070 unrestricted vectors and 147,892,558 admissible vectors. At each cap the two numbers use their respective proved rectangles, and they overlap. Both sets of counts and zero witness totals were validated per tuple and per d.

The open classification counts remain **63** through exponent four and **210** through six. Of these, 4 and 12 respectively are on the slice with the admissibility-preserving cutoff. None is removed from the open counts by this large cutoff alone.
